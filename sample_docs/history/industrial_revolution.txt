The Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution began in Britain around 1760 and changed how goods
were made. Work moved from homes and small workshops into factories.

Key inventions:
- The spinning jenny and the water frame mechanised cotton spinning.
- James Watt improved the steam engine in the 1770s, so factories no longer
  had to be built next to rivers.
- Railways and steam locomotives moved coal, goods and people quickly.

Effects: rapid growth of cities, long hours and child labour in factories,
the rise of a working class, and later reforms such as the Factory Acts.
